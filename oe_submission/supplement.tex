Reciprocity Constrains Angular Mixing in Passive Light-Extraction Structures:
Two Spectral Metrics for OLED Outcoupling

Notes S1–S4 support the spectral metrics: model details, the devices and the structure library, accuracy and convergence, and the separation of mixing from escape. Notes S5–S8 support the freeform-versus-hemisphere case study, and Note S9 reports direct ray tracing and a blind test. Main-text references are cited as "main ref. n".

\section*{Note S1 | Model details and numerical checks}

\textbf{Polarization and azimuth.} The transport model is scalar. $R(\theta)$ is the polarization average $(|r_s|^2+|r_p|^2)/2$ of the stack reflectance, $\mathbf{v}^{(0)}$ is the sum of the s- and p-polarized power launched into the substrate, and the tracers apply the unpolarized Fresnel coefficients at every encounter. Textures that are not axisymmetric (hexagonal arrays, supercells, wavy fields) are reduced to a polar-angle kernel by launching rays at the centre angle of each 1\ensuremath{^\circ} bin, uniformly over the cell and in azimuth, and binning the exit directions by polar angle only; $B_R$ is the position- and azimuth-averaged return operator.

\textbf{Full series.} $\eta_{\mathrm{sta}}$ is obtained by a direct linear solve on the full 0–90\ensuremath{^\circ} grid. Over all cases the spectral radius of $\mathrm{diag}\,\mathbf{R}\,B_R$ stays below 0.9991, the residual below $10^{-16}$, and a pass-by-pass sum agrees with the solve to $10^{-14}$.

\textbf{Angular cutoff.} Bins near 90\ensuremath{^\circ} carry almost no étendue but can return light at their own angle, and a few such states can dominate $\lambda_2$ without affecting extraction. The spectral metrics are therefore evaluated on $\theta < 85\ensuremath{^\circ}$, which excludes 0.76\% of the étendue and less than 0.1\% of $\mathbf{v}^{(0)}$: $B_R$ is restricted to these bins, $M$ is formed by renormalizing the columns of the sub-block, and $\rho$ is taken from the sub-block. $\eta_{\mathrm{sta}}$, in the full series and in the reduced model, always uses 0–90\ensuremath{^\circ}. For every structure $\lambda_2$ is real and positive (0.09–0.88), so $|\lambda_2| = \lambda_2$. $N_{\mathrm{mix}}$ is the relaxation time, not the e-folding pass number $-1/\ln\lambda_2$ (2 against 1.44 at $\lambda_2$ = 0.5).

For the freeform 40–60\ensuremath{^\circ} lens and height-randomized hemispheres the full-range value is set by grazing states. With the 85\ensuremath{^\circ} cutoff the reciprocal reduced model has a mean error of 0.30 pp and a largest error of 3.0 pp over the 342 cases, against 0.55 and 4.7 pp without it. The absolute values of $N_{\mathrm{mix}}$ and $\xi$ depend on the cutoff ($\xi$ of the hemispherical MLA is 0.65, 0.47, and 0.31 at 80\ensuremath{^\circ}, 85\ensuremath{^\circ}, and 90\ensuremath{^\circ}), but the ranking does not: the rank correlation with the 85\ensuremath{^\circ} values is 0.96–0.99 for $N_{\mathrm{mix}}$ and 0.90–0.98 for $\xi$ at 80\ensuremath{^\circ}, 88\ensuremath{^\circ}, and 90\ensuremath{^\circ} (Table S6).

\textbf{Detailed balance of the traced matrices.} The relative asymmetry $\sum|F-F^{\mathsf T}|/\sum|F|$ of $F_{ji} = w_i B_R(i\to j)$ is 0.05–0.13 on 1\ensuremath{^\circ} bins, 0.009–0.024 on 5\ensuremath{^\circ} bins, and 0.002–0.007 on 15\ensuremath{^\circ} bins, falling as expected for Monte Carlo noise; the planar matrix is exactly symmetric. Symmetrizing $F$ while keeping each column sum changes $N_{\mathrm{mix}}$ by at most 0.20 (2.7\%), $\xi$ by 0.005, $\eta_{\mathrm{sta}}$ by 0.04 pp, and the reduced-model error by 0.04 pp over all 37 non-planar structures and three devices.

\textbf{Reciprocal redistribution.} For $B_R(i\to r) = (1-p_i)[(1-m)\delta_{ir} + m q_r]$, detailed balance of the mixing term requires $w_i(1-p_i)q_r = w_r(1-p_r)q_i$, i.e. $q \propto w(1-p)$. The normalized kernel $(1-m)I + m\,q\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}$ has eigenvalues 1 and $1-m$, so $m = 1-\lambda_2$ for any $q$.

\textbf{Complete reciprocal mixing.} With $q_r = w_r(1-p_r)/\sum_k w_k(1-p_k)$, $p_{\mathrm{mix}} = \sum_r q_r p_r = (p - \langle p^2\rangle)/(1-p) = p - \sigma_p^2/(1-p)$, where $\langle\cdot\rangle$ is the étendue-weighted mean; $p_{\mathrm{mix}} \le p$ is the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality $p^2 \le \langle p^2\rangle$. The rank-one kernel $B_R = q(\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{p})^{\mathsf T}$ with $\mathrm{diag}\,\mathbf{R}$ gives $(I-\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^{\mathsf T})^{-1} = I + \mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^{\mathsf T}/(1-\mathbf{v}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{u})$ with $u_k = R_k q_k$, $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{1}-\mathbf{p}$, hence the closed form of main-text Sec. 2.4; it agrees with the numerical series to $10^{-9}$ for all 342 cases. Values for every structure are in \texttt{selectivity/data/reciprocal\_limit.json}; $p_{\mathrm{mix}}/p$ is 0.87–0.91 for hemispherical MLAs, 0.81–0.87 for freeform MLAs, 0.84–0.93 for random MLAs, 0.37–0.90 for wavy surfaces, 0.59–0.99 for scattering layers, and 0.14 for the planar interface.

\textbf{Bounds on $p_\infty$.} With $D = \mathrm{diag}(w)$, $D^{-1/2}B_R D^{1/2}$ is symmetric, so $\rho(B_R)$ is the maximum of its Rayleigh quotient. With $x = D^{1/2}\mathbf{1}$ the quotient is $\sum_{i,r} w_i B_R(i\to r)/\sum_i w_i = 1-\tilde p$, both sums running over the bins below the cutoff; hence $p_\infty \le \tilde p$. $\tilde p$ is the probability that uniform-radiance light leaves the truncated chain in one encounter, by escape or by return beyond the cutoff, and exceeds $p$ by less than 0.008. The column sums are $1-p(\theta_i)$, so $p_\infty \ge \min_\theta p(\theta)$. All 37 non-planar structures satisfy both bounds, with smallest margins of 0.0014 (upper) and 0.106 (lower); the planar interface meets the lower bound with equality. With $p$ in place of $\tilde p$ the upper bound would fail for the thick scattering layer ($\tau^*$ = 4) by 0.005. $\xi$ is a structure-only descriptor; the device-aware variant $1-\rho(\mathrm{diag}\,\mathbf{R}\,B_R)$ ranks the structures like $p_\infty$ (Spearman 0.956, Al, 550 nm). A two-parameter reciprocal kernel fitted to both $\lambda_2$ and $\rho$, and a two-rate scalar formula, were not more accurate overall than the one-parameter model (0.32 pp mean) and are not used.

\textbf{Band-resolved bound.} The substrate bins are sorted by radiance $v^{(0)}_k/w_k$; the étendue of an air band, mapped into the substrate by Snell's law, is $(\sin^2\theta_{\mathrm{hi}}-\sin^2\theta_{\mathrm{lo}})/n^2$ in units of $w$, and it is filled with the brightest bins first, the last one fractionally. The bound is lossless, polarization-independent, and integrated over azimuth. Each band's bound is a separate maximum, so the per-band bounds (sum 0.676) do not add up to the bound for the whole hemisphere (0.540), which fills the étendue $1/n^2$ once; the uniform-radiance bound is additive (0.439, of which 0.014 lies above 80\ensuremath{^\circ}).

\textbf{Height-field tracer.} The surface $z = h(x, y)$ separates the index-matched medium ($n = 1.51$) from air; rays are marched with a Lipschitz-safe step, refined by bisection, and reflected or refracted with the unpolarized Fresnel coefficients. 20,000 rays per 1\ensuremath{^\circ} incidence bin are used (10,000 for wavy surfaces). For a hemispherical lenslet the tracer reproduces the LightTools return matrix of main ref. 14: escape probability 0.4275 against 0.4274, transmittance within 0.008 at every angle, and fewer than 0.1\% of rays undetermined. The freeform profiles are rebuilt from the optimized control points with a PCHIP spline.

\textbf{Random lens arrays} tile a 6 \ensuremath{\times} 6 supercell periodically. Shape spread $s$ perturbs the five control points of the quarter circle by Gaussian noise of standard deviation $0.25s$ (radial) and $0.4s$ (height), so that $s$ = 1 approaches independent profiles; height spread scales each lenslet by $1\pm s$; position jitter displaces each centre by up to a fraction of the pitch, optionally with \ensuremath{\pm}20\% radius spread. Where lenslets overlap the higher surface is taken; profiles with near-vertical walls are rejected. \textbf{Wavy surfaces} are periodic Gaussian-correlated height fields on a 12 \ensuremath{\times} 12 box (correlation length one), scaled to a prescribed rms slope; only the slope statistics matter in ray optics. \textbf{Scattering layers} are non-absorbing, index-matched Henyey–Greenstein slabs (20,000 rays per 0.5\ensuremath{^\circ} bin) combined with the flat exit face by the adding method.

\section*{Note S9 | Direct ray tracing and blind test}

\textbf{Direct ray tracing.} Rays are launched with the first-pass distribution $\mathbf{v}^{(0)}$ (Al, 550 nm; polar angle drawn uniformly in $\sin^2\theta$ within each 1\ensuremath{^\circ} bin, azimuth and position uniform), traced through the height field, and, when returned, absorbed with probability $1-R(\theta)$ or reflected specularly, keeping their azimuth, and sent to a new uniformly drawn position (the substrate is thick compared with the lattice). $10^6$ rays per case; the standard error is 0.04–0.05 pp. A second run re-randomizes the azimuth at every pass, which is what the chain on polar angle assumes.
